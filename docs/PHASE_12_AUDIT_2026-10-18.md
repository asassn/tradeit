# Phase 12 audited, and its named deliverable built

Audited 2026-10-18, the fifth phase audited this way and the second — after
Phase 11 — where the audit finds the deliverable genuinely absent.

## The audit

The roadmap says what to expect, and it is right:

> The measurement contracts already exist […] **What does not exist is the
> framework that turns those numbers into a decision**, and it is the
> deliverable here.

| requirement | state |
|---|---|
| measurement contracts | **built** — `PerformanceMetrics` declares seventeen fields with `statistically_meaningful` and `trustworthy` as explicit guards |
| governed promotion and demotion | **built** — `strategy/lifecycle.py`, nine states, promotion one rung with a citation, demotion any distance, `LIVE` refused without the ADR-0004 interlock |
| reproducibility metadata | **built** — `RunManifest`, taken and never constructed by the engine |
| full-pipeline validation | **built** — `validation/` is 7,946 lines across eleven modules |
| **the readiness framework** | **absent** — `grep -rl readiness src/` found two unrelated files |
| strategy-decay detection | **absent** |
| kill switch, stale-data protection, duplicate-order protection | **absent** — zero matches in `src/` |
| operational runbooks, monitoring, incident response | **absent** |

So the phase is roughly half built, and the half that is missing includes the
part it is named for.

## What was built: `tradeit.readiness`

Two modules. `criteria.py` is the list of what must be shown; `assess.py`
applies it. The design is driven by the roadmap's two hard rules, and the second
one shaped everything.

### No single metric decides

Promotion is a **conjunction** of every applicable criterion, and each
criterion's verdict is reported individually — because *"it passed"* and *"it
passed nine of ten"* are different findings and the second is the one worth
reading.

**Criteria are cumulative up the ladder.** A criterion asked at `BACKTESTING` is
still asked at `LIVE`; otherwise a version could climb past evidence it no
longer has. Three criteria apply at `BACKTESTING` and thirteen at `LIVE`, and a
test asserts the count is monotonic.

**Demotion is a disjunction: any one criterion firing is enough.** The asymmetry
is deliberate and is the roadmap's — all the evidence must hold to promote, and
one broken thing is sufficient reason to stop. A framework needing unanimity to
demote would keep a decayed strategy running.

**An unmeasured demotion criterion does not demote.** Demoting a running
strategy because a monitor was not reporting would turn a broken dashboard into
a trading decision. It is surfaced instead, and a caller that wants to treat
unmonitored as unsafe reads `unresolved` and decides — that is policy and
belongs to the caller.

### No threshold chosen after seeing the result it would judge

This is easy to write and hard to enforce, because the natural way to pick a
number is to look at the output and choose one that separates good from bad.

**So it is enforced structurally.** Every threshold carries a `ThresholdSource`
and a citation, and a `Threshold` **refuses to be constructed without one**. A
threshold whose source is `UNAPPROVED` **blocks promotion rather than being
applied** — the framework declines to judge against a number nobody chose, and
says so by name.

That is the `UNRESOLVED`-is-first-class discipline turned on the framework
itself, and it has a practical consequence: **this module ships complete and
honest while several of its numbers are still owed.** The alternative designs
were both worse — leave the criterion out, and it is silently unenforced; give
it a default, and the default becomes policy by accident.

### Three states, because two would hide the useful one

A criterion is `PASS`, `FAIL` or `UNRESOLVED`. **A missing measurement is
`UNRESOLVED`, never `FAIL`:** *we measured it and it was not good enough* and
*nobody measured it* are different findings, and collapsing them would make the
fix look like "improve the strategy" when the fix is "run the test".

An `UNRESOLVED` blocks exactly as a `FAIL` does — fail closed — and is reported
separately.

## The four thresholds this framework will not invent

Each is declared, measured where a measurement exists, and **blocking**:

| criterion | what is owed |
|---|---|
| `out_of_sample_clears_hurdle` | The ledger hurdle is `small_sample_hurdle(trials, blocks)`, computed per test. Encoding a constant would contradict the ledger. Needs a decision: does readiness ask for the hurdle at assessment time, or a standing minimum? |
| `walk_forward_consistency` | `walkforward.py` computes `consistency`; nothing written says what value is good enough. Choosing one now, *after* this project's walk-forward results exist, is precisely the after-the-fact threshold the roadmap forbids. |
| `out_of_sample_degradation` | Measured twice — §52 and §53 both kept direction and lost roughly two thirds of magnitude. Two observations describe what happened; they do not establish an acceptable limit, and setting one from them would be fitting the bar to the data. |
| `live_vs_expected` (demotion) | Strategy-decay detection is a named requirement and nothing says how far live may fall below expectation before a strategy comes down. **The most consequential number in the module and the most obviously wrong to invent.** |

## The framework pointed at this project's own evidence

`scripts/readiness_report.py` assesses `ma_cross_baseline` — the only strategy
run end to end — from measurements that already exist. **Nothing was computed
for the report**, because an assessment that generated its own inputs could also
quietly choose them.

**The expected answer is HOLD, and reaching it is the check.** There is no
validated out-of-sample edge after 144 ledger trials, so a framework reporting
anything else would be broken. The script raises rather than printing if every
rung reports `PROMOTE`.

| rung | verdict |
|---|---|
| `validated` | no criteria here; deferred to `strategy/validate.py` |
| `backtesting` | **PROMOTE** — 3 pass |
| `out_of_sample_testing` | hold — 5 pass, **1 fail**, 1 unresolved |
| `paper_trading` | hold — 6 pass, 1 fail, 4 unresolved |
| `eligible_for_capital` | hold — 7 pass, 1 fail, 4 unresolved |
| `live` | hold — 7 pass, 2 fail, 4 unresolved |

**It stops at `out_of_sample_testing`, and the failing criterion is
`beats_baselines`** — because §52 and §53 both failed their held-out tests. That
is the right reason to stop and the framework found it without being told.

One rung deliberately asks nothing: `VALIDATED` is handled by
`strategy/validate.py` against the pattern vocabulary, the detector registry and
the mandate, and a second implementation here is how two rules diverge. The
report prints that as a **deferral**, not a stop — a first version showed it as
the stopping point, which read as though the framework had refused at the first
gate.

## What Phase 12 still owes

**The framework is built. The phase is not closed**, and the remaining items are
not small:

* **Four approved thresholds** (above). No further work supplies them.
* **Strategy-decay detection** — the criterion exists; the monitor that feeds it
  does not.
* **Risk controls outside a strategy's reach**: kill switch, stale-data
  protection, duplicate-order protection. The roadmap lists these explicitly and
  `grep` finds none of them. Execution reconciliation, the fourth on that list,
  was built in Phase 11.
* **Operational runbooks, monitoring, incident response.**

Target stays **2026-11-28**, and on current evidence it is achievable.

**What completing this phase will not do.** The ADR-0004 interlock stands, and a
`PROMOTE` verdict for `LIVE` is a statement that declared bars are cleared —
not an authorisation. The owner still decides, and nothing in this framework
asks to be trusted instead of him.

# Addendum, 2026-10-18 — the three controls outside a strategy's reach

The audit above listed kill switch, stale-data protection and duplicate-order
protection as absent, with zero matches in `src/`. All three are now built, in
`risk/controls.py` and enforced at the execution boundary.

## Why they are not `RiskRule`s, which was the first real decision

`RiskRule`'s docstring is explicit and load-bearing:

> Rules are pure functions of (proposal, state, config). They do not read the
> database, do not consult wall-clock time, and do not mutate anything — which
> is what lets a backtest evaluate the exact rules that would run live.

**All three controls need precisely what that forbids.** A kill switch is
operator state. Stale-data protection is a fact about when data arrived.
Duplicate protection is a fact about what has already been sent. Forcing them
into `RiskRule` would have meant weakening the one sentence that makes the risk
layer backtestable — so they are a separate layer instead.

**Enforced where orders leave**, in `BrokerExecutionService.place`, not at
sizing. That placement is the point: *a kill switch that only stopped sizing
would let a resting order fill after it was thrown.*

## What each one refuses

**`KillSwitch`** can be thrown in-process by a system that detected its own
problem, or **by a file on disk** by an operator who needs to stop a running
system and cannot wait for it to ask. The file is read on **every call**, never
cached, because a switch that was read at startup is not a switch. An empty file
still throws it — `touch STOP` is the fastest thing an operator under pressure
will do. **An unreadable file is treated as thrown**, failing closed: the
alternative is a control that stops working exactly when the filesystem is in
trouble. And a process **cannot clear a switch thrown by file** — that one is
the operator's, and clearing it would be overruling the person who threw it.

**`StaleDataGuard`** takes `max_age` with **no default**, because the roadmap
says these values *"need approval and are not inferred"* and a staleness
tolerance nobody chose is the number that will be wrong on the day the feed dies
quietly. It is judged against an `as_of` the caller supplies and **never reads
the clock**, so a backtest and a live session evaluate the same guard — a guard
nobody can replay is a guard nobody can test. Never-seen data is refused, since
that is the most stale a thing can be.

**`DuplicateOrderGuard`** refuses a resent `client_order_id`, which a venue's
idempotency already makes safe and is refused here anyway so safety does not
depend on every venue implementing it. **The case that matters is the other
one:** a *different* id for the same instrument, side and session — a system
that decides twice, doubling a position while every individual order looks
correct. That is what an idempotency key cannot catch, because the ids differ.

**Every control runs even after the first refusal.** A caller that stopped at
the first would learn the switch is thrown and not that the data is also three
days old — and the second fact explains the first.

**A refusal is journalled** as a `DECLINED` entry with every reason, through the
single journal writer.

## Three design choices worth stating

**Controls are opt-in, and `configured` reports what is installed.** Every
existing caller keeps working, visibly unprotected, rather than every backtest
in the repository acquiring a stale-data guard whose tolerance nobody chose. A
run with no controls must be *visible* as such rather than letting a reader
assume protection that was never wired.

**Installed controls make `session_date` and `as_of` required on `place`.**
Defaulting them to now would make the stale-data guard unreplayable and the
duplicate guard wrong across midnight UTC — the same defect `ruff` caught in
`reconcile` earlier today.

**A duplicate is recorded only after a successful submission.** An order the
venue refused was not sent, and recording it would block the legitimate retry.
A mutant that recorded first fails that test.

## Status

Phase 12 now has its framework and its controls. **Still owed:** the four
approved thresholds, the decay monitor that feeds `live_vs_expected`, and
operational runbooks, monitoring and incident response.

**The four thresholds are not currently blocking anything real**, and that is
worth saying plainly rather than presenting them as urgent: `ma_cross_baseline`
fails at `out_of_sample_testing` on `beats_baselines`, several rungs below where
any of them bind. They become the binding constraint the moment a strategy
actually clears that criterion, and not before.

Target stays **2026-11-28**.
