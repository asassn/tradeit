# Phase 7 audited — the scores exist, and three of five refusals had never fired

Audited 2026-10-17, the third phase audited this way in a day. **The pattern
held a third time, and then broke in a more interesting place.**

## The audit

Phase 7 requires three scores, deliberately separated — standalone, portfolio
fit, and the combination that ranks capital. All three exist.

| module | lines | tests | run on corpus data before |
|---|---|---|---|
| `strategy/base.OpportunityScore` (score 1) | — | — | yes |
| `opportunity/fit.py` (score 2) | 175 | 12, shared with `final` | **never** |
| `opportunity/final.py` (score 3) | 81 | same 12 | **never** |
| `opportunity/slate.py` | 141 | 12 | **never** |
| `opportunity/gates.py` | 147 | 11 | **never** |

583 lines, 35 tests, and **no script in the repository imported
`tradeit.opportunity` at all.** Phase 7 was not unbuilt. It was unexercised, in
exactly the way Phases 8 and 9 were.

## Why "does it run" is the wrong question here

For Phases 8 and 9, exercising a module meant handing it corpus data and
checking it did not fall over. That is not sufficient for Phase 7, because the
deliverable is not a number — it is a *distinction*:

> A great stock and a great trade for this portfolio right now are different
> questions, and only the second may authorise a trade.

**A fit score that never changes a ranking has not been exercised**, however
many tests it passes. The code would run, the numbers would look plausible, and
the claim the phase exists to support would be untested. So the measurement is:

    How often does portfolio fit actually change what gets the capital?

## The first run proved nothing, and said so

Fourteen large caps, 2010-2019: 123 candidates scored, **zero vetoed, zero
rankings changed**. The tempting conclusion is that the fit score is inert.

It is not what happened. **Only 8 of 115 slates ever held two contenders, and
the largest held two.** A slate of one cannot test a ranking. The run said
nothing about the fit score at all — it was a fact about a universe that
nominates one candidate at a time, and the script now refuses to draw a
conclusion when no slate was contested.

Recorded because the first version of the reporting would have printed "fit
changed nothing" and left the reader to conclude the module was broken.

## The real run: 143 names, 2010-2019

| | |
|---|---|
| candidates scored | 587 over 499 slates |
| admitted to capital | 485 |
| **refused by the fit** | **102 (17.4%)**, tripping 123 veto reasons between them |
| fit score | min 0.350, median 0.675, max 1.000 |
| slates with two or more contenders | 75 of 499, largest 6 |
| **slates where fit changed who ranked first** | **4 of the 75 that could** |

**The central claim is exercised, and weakly.** Four reorderings out of
seventy-five contested slates — 2012-01-20, 2016-02-29, 2017-04-27, 2019-02-01 —
is real evidence that the fit score discriminates on corpus data, and it is not
evidence that the separation earns anything. It was never going to be: the run
scores read-only and does not feed the engine's decisions, because wiring score
3 into position selection changes what the backtest measures and that is a
product decision with a return attached.

## Three of five refusals have never fired on real data

| veto reason | fired | on what |
|---|---|---|
| `position_limit_reached` | 49 | genuine |
| `heat_limit_exceeded` | 48 | genuine |
| `sector_limit_reached` | 26 | **all 26 on the pooled `UNCLASSIFIED` bucket** |
| `already_at_full_size` | 0 | unit tests only |
| `insufficient_cash` | 0 | unit tests only |

**The sector veto is the finding.** It fired 26 times, and **every one fell on a
candidate with no SIC classification**, pooled into the single `UNCLASSIFIED`
bucket that the 30% cap also limits. Zero fell on a named sector. So the sector
veto has still **never once fired on real sector concentration** — it fired on a
coverage gap, and without the attribution it would have entered this record as
"the sector veto works on real data."

Same family as §41 and the rest of *Never answer by accident*: the expected
answer arrived, and it was not an answer to the question asked. The attribution
was added before the number was written down, not after.

**Why coverage is so much worse here than corpus-wide.** 48 of 143 names
classified (33.6%) against 76.4% corpus-wide. The 143 were chosen for *price*
coverage over 2010-2019, which selects differently from SIC reachability. Not
investigated further — it is a sampling fact about this universe, not a defect
in the sector map.

## What is owed before Phase 7 can be called complete

**Two roadmap gates sit in front of it and neither is finished.** The
multi-timeframe gate still owes the `intraday-01` corpus, the `bar_status`
contract (deliberately deferred) and the extended-hours declaration; the
strategy-builder gate still owes composition from the capability registries and
persistence of versions.

**The intraday corpus is not optional and not a formality.** Phase 7 scores
setups per horizon, and **the Day mandate cannot be scored at all without
intraday bars.** Everything measured above is Swing/`1d`. Declaring Phase 7
complete on Daily evidence alone would collapse Day into Swing, which is the one
thing the three-horizon architecture exists to prevent.

So the honest status is:

* **Phase 7 for Swing: built, exercised, and demonstrably discriminating** on
  587 real candidates — with the sector veto unexercised and two veto reasons
  untouched.
* **Phase 7 for Day: cannot begin.** No intraday corpus exists.
* **Phase 7 for Retirement: untested.** Nothing blocks it; nobody has run it.

## One inconsistency worth fixing

`opportunity/gates.py` exports `breakout_confirmation_gate` in its own
`__all__`, and the package's `__init__.py` does not re-export it — so the one
blocking gate in the phase is the one thing a caller cannot import from
`tradeit.opportunity`. Left alone for now rather than changed as a drive-by,
and recorded here so it is not rediscovered.

## Status

**Phase 7 is not closed, and this is the first of the three audits that did not
end in a closure.** The difference is not effort or code: it is that Phase 7's
completion depends on a corpus that does not exist, where 8 and 9 depended only
on being run. Track A's Phase 7 target of 2026-11-07 is **achievable for Swing
and unachievable for Day**, and the plan should say which it means.
